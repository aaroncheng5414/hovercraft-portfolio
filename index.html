Our working prototype / Makeathon 2023
Makeathon 2023Accessible design

An easier way in.

I helped build a motion-sensed door prototype with visual and auditory cues. Our team combined a servo-driven door with a sensor, lights, and a buzzer to make its operation easier to notice.

Mechanical designLaser cutting + 3D printingSensors + servos
Inside the build +

From CAD to a moving prototype

Our team designed the door, frame, and sensor housing in CAD. We laser-cut the structure, printed mounts and hinges, soldered the electronics, and tested the code with the assembled door.

Design choices

We considered the opening and closing delay, servo position, sensor angle, and the placement of visual and auditory cues.

Prototype photo and CAD images from our team’s Makeathon 2023 presentation.

An accessibility-focused prototype, not a certified or deployed door system.

A window that
moves with you.

I’m working with a team on a virtual window that changes perspective as the viewer moves. Our proposal brings camera-based position and head tracking together with a screen, embedded control, and FPGA acceleration.

Spatial interactionComputer visionIn progress
The planned experience +

A camera tracks the viewer’s position. A rendered 3D scene adjusts to that viewpoint, giving the screen the feel of a window into another space.

Our proposed system uses a microcontroller for coordination and an FPGA for intensive vision processing, with C/C++, Python, Verilog, and raycasting under consideration.

Current senior design work. These are planned capabilities, not completed performance results. The image is a concept illustration.

Custom headset parts / CAD render
VR Pokémon / Embedded labSpring 2026

A game that crosses
into hardware.

I designed custom headset parts and contributed gameplay, packet parsing, audio, and raycasting for our team’s VR Pokémon project. The system connects a handheld controller, TM4C, Raspberry Pi, and two displays.

Headset mechanicsC++ / PythonGraphics + embedded systems
Inside the build +

My contribution

I worked on the headset housing, LCD panel, and electronics-holder designs alongside firmware and rendering changes. The mechanical design builds on an existing VR-headset reference with custom parts.

Software and interaction

The code includes free roam, catching, battles, inventory, stereo raycasting, and audio. Joystick input controls movement while head orientation changes the view.

Hardware integration

A wireless controller feeds the headset’s TM4C, which communicates with a Raspberry Pi for rendering. The project also integrates an IMU, displays, and speaker output.

CAD and implementation are documented in the project. Final assembled-demo performance and frame rate are not claimed here.
